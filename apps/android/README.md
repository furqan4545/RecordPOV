# RecordPOV for Android

A native Kotlin / Jetpack Compose app for recording reactions and screen walkthroughs. The UI uses the supplied RecordPOV mark, warm paper surfaces, a single lime accent, compact controls, and direct camera manipulation. Create starts with Add a video; My videos holds saved recordings/drafts; Screen opens screen recording. The public brand/domain are RecordPOV and recordpov.app. Package IDs, preference keys, notification channel IDs, and draft formats retain compatibility. New Gallery exports use Movies/RecordPOV; recovery also finds older exports in the previous folder.

## Build

Requires JDK 17+, Android SDK 37, and Build Tools 36.0.0 or newer. The Gradle wrapper is included.

```sh
./gradlew :app:assembleDebug :app:testDebugUnitTest
```

The debug APK is `app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. Install it on an existing device with `adb install -r app/build/outputs/apk/debug/app-debug.apk`. No emulator is created by this project.

## Record a reaction

1. Paste an Instagram, YouTube, X, or TikTok link and tap **Download & open video**, or tap **Upload video or photo**.
   The existing button shows **Downloading…** while the service resolves/downloads the source, with “Please wait, we’re downloading your video.” below it. The button stays on Downloading through the phone transfer. **Loading…** / “Loading your video…” cover local validation, optional sample-copy muxing, durable saving, and native player preparation. These states follow real work; cache hits add no artificial delay. Errors retain the entered link, and cancellation does not become a download error.
2. Tap **Add yourself** to grant camera/microphone access at the point it is needed. The first camera style is Square, with background removal off. No style is selected while the camera is off; later off/on cycles preserve your choice. Changing the layout resets the camera to Square. Background removal starts only when you explicitly select Cutout.
3. Drag, pinch, and rotate the camera. Choose Rounded, Circle, Square, or Cutout. The flip button switches front/back cameras and confirms the new lens only after its first frame arrives; rear-camera output is not mirrored. Regular shapes fill edge to edge with a centered camera crop and no padding. The Wider / Closer slider adjusts framing independently of the outer PiP size, from the widest filled view to 2.5× closer. Cutout keeps the full camera frame with transparent margins and allows wider framing down to .55×. **Reset** restores framing to 1× without changing the PiP position, size, rotation, or style. Switching shapes preserves a wider Cutout setting; regular shapes show it clamped to their filled minimum.
4. Choose **Timer → Off / 3s / 5s / 10s** and tap **Record**. The default is 3 seconds and your choice is remembered. The visible countdown can be cancelled without creating a take. Once recording starts, the source video plays from its current position (or rewinds if already finished). Pause, resume, or seek the source independently while the camera and narration keep recording. At the source's end, its last frame stays visible until you finish manually.
5. Add photos with the image button. The encoder pauses while the system picker is open and resumes into the same file; picker time is omitted. The source tray switches between imported photos and the original video, retaining its last playback position.
6. **Finish recording** pauses the source and opens the editor. Drag the thumbnail timeline's start/end handles to keep one continuous section, or scrub its playhead to inspect a frame. Both edges immediately seek the original capture while dragging; playback pauses and pending seeks keep only the latest target. Releasing an edge commits once and rebuilds the edited preview after a 250ms debounce, without blocking further timeline scrubbing. **Reset trim** restores the full take. **Save** exports exactly the selected start-to-end section to Gallery / Movies / RecordPOV and retains an app copy. Adjust source/microphone/music gains, import music, and review captions independently. Gallery failures preserve the app copy and draft. No Gallery copy is created when recording first finishes.

There are no mark-cut or split/delete controls. New trim gestures clear legacy internal cuts. Older invalid trims/cuts remain visibly recoverable with Reset trim; Save is disabled until one valid contiguous section of at least 250ms is selected. Audio stems, music, caption cues, and the original capture remain non-destructive.

The layout menu supports **PiP**, **Split**, and **You on stage** before and during recording. Split puts the original above the camera; drag the divider between 20% and 80%. You on stage makes the camera the main view and lets you drag/pinch the original-video overlay. The fullscreen camera toggle temporarily overrides the selected layout and restores it when toggled back, retaining PiP position, size, rotation, and framing. Stage preview and encoded frames use one renderer and geometry implementation.

The output is a portrait 720p H.264/AAC MP4. Recording retains the original camera/composited video and microphone capture. Played source assets are copied into the draft before entering the editor, so interrupted preparation and later reopening do not depend on resolver cache or external documents. The editor decodes an isolated microphone WAV, and builds a separate source WAV digitally from the source play/pause/seek timeline, with silence across reaction-only intervals. PCM presentation timestamps preserve initial track delays and gaps. A block-based PCM mixer applies independent source/mic/music gains and a final sample limiter. The video export removes the capture's original audio and muxes only this single mixed stem, preventing duplicate digital mixing. Original capture, isolated stems, music, cuts, caption cues, and gain choices remain in an app-private JSON draft, reopenable from My videos. Every edit is saved atomically; Back retains the draft and the library reloads its latest edits. Audio-preparation errors retain the original capture and offer retry rather than silently omitting a stem.

Controls use compact 40/44dp secondary targets, 48dp primary buttons, a 64dp recording control, and a horizontal camera-style selector.

## Microphone quality

Studio and Screen recording use Android's [voice-communication microphone source](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaRecorder.AudioSource#VOICE_COMMUNICATION) and request live [echo cancellation](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/audiofx/AcousticEchoCanceler) plus [noise suppression](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/audiofx/NoiseSuppressor) on the recorder's own active audio session. Recording-configuration/routing callbacks and active-session checks rebind effects after a session/route change. Actual effect readback or the recorder's [enabled-effect descriptors](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/AudioRecordingConfiguration#getClientEffects()) verify status; availability alone never counts as enabled. Pause/resume/stop/abort release or recreate the correct session effects. Studio shows the verified state; Screen recording's recording notification does too. No global audio mode, output route, source-video volume, or extra automatic gain boost is applied.

**Clean voice** in the editor is a reversible, microphone-only cleanup: a 70Hz rumble high-pass and conservative stationary-hiss attenuation capped around 6dB (about 1.1dB maximum in the 100–4500Hz voice band). There is no hard gate, normalization, or speech/background-isolation model. New takes enable it; old drafts missing the saved flag retain their original sound until you opt in. A content-hash-validated derived WAV is shared by preview/export. The captured microphone WAV is never overwritten and remains the captions input; source/music stems stay untouched. The switch can return the mix to the recorded microphone immediately on the next preview/export.

Engine support and echo-reference quality vary by phone. Enabled processing cannot guarantee removal of video speech already picked up acoustically by the microphone. Headphones are the reliable way to prevent speaker bleed; the app recommends them without requiring them.

Gallery export uses MediaStore's pending-write transaction and publishes only after the copy completes. An export ledger, shared mutex, and recovery lookup prevent duplicate copies across retries/restarts. On Android 10+ (the app's minimum), saving the app's own new media does not require broad storage access; see [Android shared-media guidance](https://developer.android.com/training/data-storage/shared/media). Storage/provider failures are still reported.

**Cutout** uses the bundled ML Kit selfie-segmentation model in stream mode. Camera frames and segmentation masks stay on the device. The model is available offline. Raw confidence masks are upscaled with pixel-center bilinear sampling, preserving fractional hair/edge alpha without thresholding or contrast hardening. Camera orientation/mirroring is applied once before inference; preview and export receive the same resulting matte. Native stream-mode smoothing is retained, with detector history reset when switching cameras or re-entering Cutout.

The pinned `16.0.0-beta6` is still the latest published bundled Android selfie-segmentation release in [Google Maven metadata](https://dl.google.com/dl/android/maven2/com/google/mlkit/segmentation-selfie/maven-metadata.xml) and [Google's release notes](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/release-notes), checked November 4, 2026. The [Android guide](https://developers.google.com/ml-kit/vision/selfie-segmentation/android) documents stream-mode smoothing and raw masks. This is a real-time segmentation model, not a claim of studio-grade hair matting; fine hair, translucent objects, motion, and similar foreground/background colors still need physical-device visual evaluation.

## Screen recording

Open the dedicated **Screen** bottom tab to start screen recording. Android requests camera/microphone access, optional notification access, display-over-other-apps permission, and a fresh system screen-capture consent for each recording. A foreground service records the entire display while a visible, movable camera overlay stays over other apps. Pinch the overlay to resize it, rotate with two fingers, or use its shape and reset buttons. Stop from the floating button, ongoing notification, or Screen recording screen. Finished Screen recordings become editable drafts; open Roast to finish the mix and export with Save. Screen recording starts after Android's capture consent; Studio's configurable countdown applies to reaction recording.

Screen recording records microphone narration. It does not digitally capture other apps' playback audio. Apps/windows that prohibit screen capture may appear blank; Android enforces those restrictions. Capture and camera behavior must be tested on physical hardware before release.

## On-device captions

Generate captions downloads a **104 MB multilingual Whisper tiny int8 model** on the first explicit action. There is no startup/prefetch/background model download. Progress and cancellation are visible; verified model files remain available offline. Native inference finishes its current speech segment before cancellation completes. The caption backend opens only `microphone.wav`, before any music or source mix; it never opens the live microphone or uploads audio. Android SpeechRecognizer is intentionally unused because its [external-audio API can fall back to live microphone input](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/speech/RecognizerIntent#EXTRA_AUDIO_SOURCE).

The CPU runtime is the official [sherpa-onnx 2.0.1 Android AAR](https://github.com/k2-fsa/sherpa-onnx/releases/tag/v2.0.1), SHA256 `a8d09d1d32f3eb8520482c6d3d43fae674681c5b050385967c70a3c2c9bf0ee7`. Converted model files are pinned to [upstream commit 65176e2](https://huggingface.co/csukuangfj/sherpa-onnx-whisper-tiny/tree/65176e2deb88badc814a94058666cadccc29b61c); exact byte lengths and SHA256 checks are enforced before inference. Runtime/model notices are retained under `licenses/` and packaged as app assets. Sherpa is Apache 2.0; upstream [Whisper code and weights are MIT](https://github.com/openai/whisper#license).

The standard tiny model does not supply attention outputs needed for word timestamps through this runtime. Cues therefore use measured microphone activity/silence intervals, split into phrases of at most six seconds; no word timestamps are fabricated. Cues are editable and remap through the same trim/cut ranges as video/audio. Review transcription and phrase timing before exporting. English, Urdu, Hindi, Korean, and automatic detection are offered; tiny-model accuracy varies by speech/language and noise.

## Optional account

**Settings → Account** is the only account entry point. Recording, editing, importing and Gallery export do not require an account. Local sessions restore without launching a provider; opening Account checks the server configuration/session. Provider buttons appear only when `/v1/auth/config` enables their actual credentials. Configuration/network failures leave recording available. There is no Supabase or Firebase dependency, media upload, automatic sign-in prompt, or cloud project sync.

Google uses the official [Credential Manager implementation](https://developer.android.com/identity/sign-in/credential-manager-siwg-implementation), stable `credentials` / `credentials-play-services-auth` **1.6.0**, and Google ID **1.2.0**. Google Maven lists 1.2.1 as newest, but its Kotlin 2.4 metadata is incompatible with this project's Kotlin 2.2 compiler; 1.2.0 is the compatible stable pin. The explicit Sign in with Google button requests a nonce-bound ID token for the **web/server client ID from public config**, then exchanges it with the HTTPS account service. No Google client secret belongs in the app. Only basic identity is requested; sign-in does not authorize YouTube access.

Apple on Android uses official Browser Custom Tabs **1.10.0**, an app-generated PKCE verifier/state, and the server's same-origin browser flow. The return is the verified HTTPS App Link `https://api.recordpov.app/auth/apple/return`; origin, exact path, unique query fields, matching state, and pending-request expiry are checked before exchanging its one-use code. Pending requests survive process recreation in encrypted storage. Duplicate returns cannot repeat a successful exchange; callbacks wait for an ongoing account refresh rather than being dropped. Cancel sign-in or deletion clears pending browser authorization, and deletion reauthentication must match the original user. No embedded WebView or Apple private signing key is used. The Apple button remains hidden until the server advertises its real configured browser capability.

The server session and pending PKCE material use AES-256-GCM with fresh random IVs and purpose-bound authenticated data; the non-exportable key is generated by [AndroidKeyStore](https://developer.android.com/privacy-and-security/keystore). Only encrypted envelopes enter separate account preferences. Provider ID tokens are not persisted. Bearer sessions go only to the fixed HTTPS account API, never the configurable media-service address; redirects are disabled. Sessions/tokens are redacted from diagnostic string representations. Sign out revokes the remote session when reachable and clears this phone's session/provider state. Delete account requires confirmation and recent reauthentication when requested by the server. Both operations preserve local recordings, stems, drafts, and settings.

- Google Android OAuth SHA-1: `AE:F1:60:A1:DA:98:03:94:29:F5:E2:61:3E:3C:CF:21:E3:5C:AE:CF`.

These are the public certificate fingerprints from the current local debug keystore. A signed release/Google Play build needs its actual distribution certificate registered separately; it cannot reuse the debug fingerprint. The server must publish the matching package/SHA-256 in its HTTPS `/.well-known/assetlinks.json`. Provider registration, consent screen, policy/support details, Apple Services ID/key, and domain association are server/console setup. They cannot be validated by a unit test; no physical Android provider sign-in is claimed.

Google's G and Apple's generated sign-in button are unmodified official assets from [Google Identity](https://developers.google.com/identity/branding-guidelines) and [Apple's button endpoint](https://appleid.cdn-apple.com/appleid/button?type=sign-in&color=black&border=false&border_radius=12&width=300&height=48&locale=en_US).

## Video service

Local photos/videos work without a server. Social links use the repository's `server/` resolver. Imported clips are validated and saved into durable app-private files before Studio opens, so playback/export does not depend on expiring CDN links or an evictable cache. My videos → Recent imports reopens them later.

The default service is **`https://api.recordpov.app`**, using the normal HTTPS client without an admin bearer token. A one-time SharedPreferences migration replaces the former `http://10.0.2.2:8787` emulator default and known `http://192.168.200.160:8787` Mac default. Intentional custom public addresses and different local hosts/ports/paths stay unchanged. The migration marker is also retained when Settings saves an address, so a later deliberate local-development choice is not rewritten on the next launch.

The app first requests `POST /v2/media/resolve`. A prepared response downloads its same-origin API file. A direct response contains either one complete progressive MP4 or exactly one H.264 video file plus one AAC audio file. The phone downloads split assets concurrently and uses Android MediaMuxer to copy compressed samples into MP4, without decoding/reencoding or dropping tracks. A common presentation clock retains audio/video offsets and AVC B-frame ordering; samples are interleaved while preserving each track's extractor order. Local MediaExtractor/metadata inspection checks actual H.264/AAC tracks, SPS eight-bit 4:2:0 through level5.2, rotation, duration ≤15 minutes, raster ≤3840 long /2160 short, frame rate ≤60, expected dimensions/duration, and required audio. Unsupported/expired/denied/direct-validation/mux errors fall back to v1 exactly once; cancellation is never retried. A missing v2 endpoint also falls back. Each YouTube resolve attempt creates a fresh capability/session; tickets are not reused.

CDN requests allow only HTTPS 443, the exact platform CDN host policy in the shared server contract, and User-Agent/Referer/Origin/Accept headers. Authorization, cookies, and YouTube session headers cannot enter the CDN header set. Every redirect is rechecked; all local DNS answers must be public before OkHttp connects to the resulting numeric addresses. API prepared-file redirects must stay on the configured API origin and validated file path. Downloads have combined 2GiB direct/4GB prepared byte bounds, per-call timeouts (60-second idle reads, 2-hour calls, 930-second non-YouTube resolves), a 2-hour-30-second direct-transfer wall limit, and a 3-hour whole-job bound. Signed asset URLs/headers stay only in memory and are redacted from diagnostics. No HLS/DASH manifest or segment fetching is attempted.

YouTube extraction still runs on the server through the phone's bounded TLS relay, using the official OkHttp 5.5.1 transport and the existing destination/credit restrictions. The relay closes after the v2/v1 manifest response, before direct/prepared media transfer. No platform TLS interception, account cookies, permanent app secrets, or extra Google scopes are used. The existing normal resolver remains available for prepared fallback. Backgrounding no longer cancels a service-owned YouTube job; network changes, expiry, cancellation, or peer failure still close its sockets. Physical Android dual-stack behavior remains unverified.

## Background imports and Recent imports

The **Download & open video** action starts a `dataSync` foreground service. Its active-only notification includes Cancel import; the UI keeps the two actual loading phases. Camera/microphone access is not involved. A bounded partial wake lock and declared foreground-service permissions keep work independent of Activity/Compose lifetime. Android 15+ timeout callbacks stop the service promptly. Notification-drawer visibility follows the phone's notification permission; the service does not require an extra permission tap before importing.

A private, atomic, expiring job descriptor stores only the requested social URL or persisted local document URI, API origin, phase, fallback state, and completed import ID. CDN URLs, headers, and session tickets are never persisted there. OS-redelivered work can restart an interrupted import with fresh metadata, up to two automatic executions within seven hours. Partial network files are discarded rather than incorrectly treated as complete or claiming byte-range resume. An interrupted/force-stopped job that Android does not restart can be started again from the retained URL or by selecting the local video again. Force-stop, storage exhaustion, provider URI grants, network policy, and OEM battery restrictions remain OS constraints.

A completion opens Studio automatically only if the original Create request stayed foreground and the user did not switch work. The Home card only adds media: a URL field, **Download & open video**, an “or” divider, and **Upload video or photo**. Downloading/Loading and explicit cancellation stay attached to that import action. A separate vertically scrolling two-column **Recent** thumbnail grid combines imported videos, unfinished current draft revisions, and saved exports by modification/import time. Imported cards reopen Studio, drafts open the editor, and saved videos use the existing player flow. Completing a background download adds it to Recent without launching an Activity or replacing another Studio/editor. Long-press a Recent card for **Delete permanently**. This removes only the selected app-owned import, draft, or export; other projects, source records, and Gallery copies are kept. My videos uses the same unified vertical grid, without separate imported/draft sections or a second delete confirmation. Saved-video Share and Gallery-save retry remain in its card context menu. Link imports and picked local videos are copied, fsynced and atomically published under private `files/imports`, with durable per-item manifests and content/source identity deduplication. Completed imports remain until explicit deletion. Deleting a recent import removes only its owned directory; the external original and draft-pinned assets remain intact. Local video imports retain their original codec/quality rather than forcing the CDN codec policy onto selected files.

For local development, change **Settings → Video service** to your reachable server address. Debug builds allow local HTTP; release builds require HTTPS. Drafts, local projects, recordings, and stems remain app-private on the phone. Camera, microphone, and screen content are not sent to the resolver; R2 is not used by the app.

## Validation and current boundaries

Source-quality imports now accept original AVC eight-bit 4:2:0 through level 5.2, up to 3840 pixels on the long side and 2160 on the short side, and actual sample timing through 60 fps. Split AAC/video sources keep encoded samples and presentation timestamps; there is no native import reencode or resolution/frame-rate reduction. The 3600-second (plus 0.2-second measurement slack)/2 GiB network bounds apply. URL re-import reuse requires the server's `qualityVersion >= 2`; older records remain available offline in Recent and coexist with a fresh version instead of being overwritten. Studio places the native decoder TextureView in the PiP, split, or stage source rectangle and draws the camera separately, removing the old 720p bitmap that covered native playback. Pausing and source-end hold retain the native frame. Bitmap readback is used only for the existing reaction recorder, whose output remains 720×1280 at 24 fps/4 Mbps. 4K60 playback depends on the device's hardware decoder and can fail or stutter on older phones; no physical-device quality check was performed. Recent thumbnails alone use a bounded 512px raster. Android capabilities are device-specific ([VideoCapabilities](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaCodecInfo.VideoCapabilities)); split import uses sample-copy muxing supported by [MediaMuxer](https://developer.android.com/reference/android/media/MediaMuxer).
